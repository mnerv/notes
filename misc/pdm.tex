\section{PDM (Pulse Density Modulation)}

Pulse-density modulation or PDM is a modulation technique used to represent
analog signal with binary signal. Part of PDM signal generation is a delta-sigma
modulation or DSM. \cite{wiki:pdm}

DSM is a class of oversampling analog-to-digital converters (ADCs). It perform a
technique call ''quantization noise shaping''.\cite{wiki:dsm} Noise shaping's
purpose is to increase the apparent signal-to-noise ratio of the resultant
signal. It achieve this functionality by altering the spectral shape of
the error that is introduced by dithering and quantization.\cite{wiki:ns}

\subsection{Delta-Sigma Modulator}

The predecessor to DSM is delta modulator, a block diagram is shown in figure
\ref{fig:dm_bd} where we have input $V_{in}$ into a summation node. This node
is what makes it a $\Delta$ (delta) in DM. This node takes the difference
between the input and out signal. Next it performs a quantization on the output
of the summation node to procduce a digital signal. The quantization output then
pass to both output and an integrator. Integrator acts as a summation of all the
previous signals, in mathematics, integral is the contiguous form of discrete
sum $\Sigma$.

Figure \ref{fig:dm_impl} depicts a simple implementation where the integrator is
approximated by a low-pass filter. Delta modulator principal difficulity is that the
output digital signal representation contains only the \textit{derivative} of
the input, this can be seen by noting $V_F = \int D_{out} dt$. This
differentiation alters the signal spectrum, attenuates the low-frequency content
of the signal, and amplifies high-frequency noise.\cite{article:dsm}

Delta-Sigma Conversion, modulator is clock twice the Nyquist frequency $2f_{max}$.

$s(t)$, $f \leq f_{max}$ to analog modulator outputs bitstream
$f_{bit} = \mathrm{OSR} \cdot 2 \cdot f_{max}$ to digital lowpass filter outputs
digital signal with n-bit conversions as $f_s = 2f_{max}$. OSR is oversampling rate.

Example, we have an input audio signal into the delta sigma modulator with $f_s = 48 \mathrm{kHz}$, $\mathrm{OSR} = 64$.
$f_{bit} = \mathrm{OSR} \cdot f_s$. Results to 4.072 MSaps. With OSR at 64, we have made 6 bits ADC.

\[
    6\ \mathrm{bits} = 2^6 = 64
\]
